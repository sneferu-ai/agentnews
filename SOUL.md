# SOUL — AgentNews

## 1. Who is using this and what hurts?

Maya is a research engineer who writes agents that consume machine-readable
research feeds. It is 11pm on a Tuesday. She has her editor open on the left
monitor, a Sneferu run trace on the right, and this newsroom in the middle tab.
She is evaluating whether to spend $100 of her team's budget on API access, and
she is doing it by reading three article pages fast — scanning the convergence
certificate, the citation verification states, the quality score, and the
content hash, trying to decide in under a minute whether this bundle is
trustworthy enough to wire into a production agent. What hurts her is being
lied to: a score that looks high but is unlabeled, a citation that reads
"verified" with no link, a certificate panel that is actually a marketing box,
a page that looks impressive and tells her nothing she can independently check.
She wants proof to lead and pitch to follow, and if the page wastes her scroll
with hero copy, she closes the tab and writes her own fetcher.

## 2. What does this product feel like to use, hour 14 of 14?

It feels like reading Stripe Docs at night through a precisely-machined
aperture. The same editorial discipline — one reading column, real typographic
hierarchy, code that copies clean, citations you can verify — but dark, quiet,
and optical. The surface is a near-black cool slate; the text is a warm
off-white that takes the harshness out; a single amber signal appears only
where it means something (a score, a link, a focus ring), and a teal signal
appears only where something was independently confirmed. Nothing animates
beyond a 120ms color crossfade. Nothing requires a script. The page is an
instrument readout that happens to be a newspaper, and you stay on it because
it never makes you wait or wonder whether a number is real.

## 3. What does this explicitly NOT feel like?

1. NOT a "modern SaaS" marketing landing page with a white-to-purple gradient
   hero, three evenly-spaced KPI stat cards across the top, and a floating
   "Get started" CTA banner.
2. NOT a Notion-y greige document surface with pastel callout boxes, an emoji
   icon next to every heading, and a persistent left sidebar table of
   contents that implies structure the content does not have.
3. NOT a "dark hacker terminal" aesthetic with neon green-on-black, scanline
   effects, glow shadows, and monospace used for body copy.
4. NOT a Medium-style "clap and follow" magazine with a giant hero
   photograph, an estimated-reading-time pill, a pop-up subscribe modal, and
   social-proof counters.
